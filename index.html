Digital Business Services · Warwick Business School · Jul 2026
Sainsbury's SmartShop Exit Redesign
Self-directed

SmartShop has a critical gap at the verification exit: no way to call a colleague. Drawing on shop-floor experience, I redesigned the flow around an ML risk-scoring layer, a digital colleague-alert in the app, and operational changes to unify service across store formats.

Technology Acceptance Model Supervised ML Task-Technology Fit Agile Scrum MoSCoW PESTEL Kano Model UK GDPR

Problem identified

  • No in-app way to signal a colleague. Customers abandon baskets hunting for staff.
  • Random blanket checks erode trust and suppress adoption.
  • Three store-format fragments: inconsistent handset availability, Nectar dependency, and bespoke exit hardware.

Proposed solution

  • ML model predicts check-worthiness at payment, waving most customers through.
  • Digital colleague-alert in the app, removing the need to hunt for staff.
  • Three enabling changes: standardise app and handset, unify Nectar flow, standardise exit around the app layer.

Implementation

  • Agile Scrum with phased rollout. Pilot runs in shadow mode (scoring without acting) before live comparison.
  • MoSCoW: colleague alert and check-worthiness model as Must-Have.
  • Centaur design (human always decides) manages false-positive risk and GDPR Article 22 exposure.
MSc Dissertation · Warwick Business School · Jan–Sep 2026
AI Adoption and Sales Efficiency in B2B SaaS

Staggered difference-in-differences across 200 B2B SaaS firms, testing whether AI adoption in go-to-market functions improves sales efficiency. Panel data in Python; treatment effects isolated at firm level. What this required: assembling the panel dataset from scratch, designing a causal inference framework, and the discipline to publish a result that contradicts what most leadership teams currently believe about AI's productivity impact.

The result: a robust null. AI adoption showed no statistically significant effect on sales efficiency once firm size and sector were controlled for. That is worth sitting with. It does not mean AI is ineffective. It means the productivity gains that leadership teams are currently approving budgets around are not yet visible in firm-level efficiency metrics. Whether that is a measurement lag, an implementation problem, or a genuine absence is the question this study opens, not closes.

International Module · HEC Paris & Station F · May 2026
Data Science & AI for Business

Applied ML, convolutional neural networks (ResNet-50), LSTM, and reinforcement learning to a retail optimisation case study: a three-layer deep learning system using overhead cameras and loyalty card data to reduce basket abandonment across a grocery estate. Business case projected ~£90M incremental annual revenue from a 2% lift in basket completion across a 500-store estate.

The consistent lesson: the bottleneck is never the model. It is framing the business question precisely enough that the model can help answer it. A well-specified simple model outperforms a complex one trained on the wrong objective almost every time. That reorientation from asking what the algorithm can do to asking what decision we are actually trying to make was the most transferable thing the programme taught.
